> **Found:** With all T130 fixes on, six pause-menu visits ran without a crash, and closing the menu stuttered less (worst frame about 130-260 ms instead of 320-470 ms), most likely from EDEN_UP_AREA_BUDGET.
>
> **So:** Test the area budget on its own on the menu route, and run a long session with fights to see if memory wears down.

Batch `eglantine-logs-2026-11-05/auto-20261112-002017-batch-t130-allfixes-menu`, plan `batch-t130-allfixes-menu.json`. Read once, then checked a second time; quote the second check.

## In plain words (written for the project owner) (second check)

All four test runs opened and closed the pause menu six times in the field and finished without a crash. Two runs had the fixes on and two had them off.

With the fixes on, closing the menu stuttered less. The worst frame was about 130-260 ms instead of about 320-470 ms, in 11 of 12 closes. A frame is one picture on screen, and its time in ms is how long it stayed up. The likely cause is the fix that stops the emulator from throwing away and rebuilding about a thousand textures (stored pictures) every time the menu closes. In the one fix close where that fix ran out of room, the stutter was back at 454 ms. All 8 fixes were on together, though, and there were only 2 runs each, so this is a strong lead, not proof.

The memory fixes for your crash never had to act. Memory got tightest during the menu visits, not during loading, but the largest free piece never went below about 230 MB. Yours was 1 MB when it died. Speed was the same either way, about 15 fps walking in the field.

1. Test the menu fix on its own against off on the same menu route (3 runs each).
2. Run a 10-minute field session with fights, fixes on against off, to see if memory wears down the way it did for you.
3. Check the odd shapes and the two short white flashes with video clips.

## Verdict (second check)

Within what this route could see, nothing went wrong with the fixes, and one gain looks likely. All 4 runs (2 with fixes, 2 controls) did all six menu visits and reached the script end with no crash, freeze or memory refusal. fps was the same: about 15 in the field, mean 27.5-28.1.

Likely gain: the menu-close stutter. The worst frame per close was 126-257 ms in 11 of 12 fix closes, against 316-468 ms in 12 of 12 control closes. This matches the mechanism of AREA_BUDGET, the fix that limits how many textures are deleted after a burst of new ones. Controls rebuilt 896-1079 textures at every close. The fix runs rebuilt 0-421, except f2 visit 5, where the budget had run down to 16/12 MiB, 1067 were rebuilt and the close took 454 ms. With 2 runs per arm and 8 switches on together, this is a strong lead. It does not prove that AREA_BUDGET alone is the cause.

Memory got tightest during the menu visits in both arms, not at the load. The largest free piece (the biggest single block of free memory) bottomed at 281/285 MiB with fixes and 267/231 MiB without. That gap is too small to call with 2 runs. The reserve (memory kept back so the game's own writes never find the pool empty) dropped to its floor of 32 blocks (64 MiB) around the first menu visit and stayed there.

The crash fixes were not tested. HEAP_STEAL and NEW_WAIT never fired, the JIT (the part that translates game code) never waited, and the transcode limits have no counter. A fix that never fired proves nothing about the owner's crash.

Not visible to this test:
- the owner's failure (largest free piece 1 MiB, reserve under its floor), after about 9 minutes with a fight
- run-to-run spread with only 2 runs per arm
- whether the bars, wedges and heart shapes in the stills are emulator faults or the game's own effects

### Did the fixes act

Acted: AREA_BUDGET clearly. In the fix runs, 10-12 'return' clean-ups were vetoed and deletion was capped (600-757 passes). Fix runs deleted 2178 and 2590 MiB in all, against 8408 and 7726 MiB in the controls. Each control menu close remade about 900-1080 images, against mostly 100-420 with the budget. ASTC_SCAN_CHUNKS fired: about 1400-1600 scans per run, about 200 MiB read and about 600 MiB skipped, but verify was off. JIT_SITE_SHARDS is a layout change and is always active: it stayed in 128 tables, max 248 KiB per grow, never over 2 MiB. PREP_UNREAD_HANDLE barely had work: boot had already built all known pipelines (0 left to prepare). So there was 1 reuse in f1, during the load, and 0 in f2. Never fired: HEAP_STEAL and NEW_WAIT. They print only their start line here, with no counter line in either fix run. TRANSCODE_BOUND and TRANSCODE_PENDING have no counter, so it is unknown what they did. As on the exit walk, the four switches aimed at heap and new-request failures were never tested. Nothing refused memory (HEAP_MID refused 0, 0 bad_alloc, 0 image or buffer refusals, 0 driver no-memory lines).

### Memory

Tighter than the exit walk, but nowhere near the owner's crash. The lowest 'largest free piece' was 231-285 MiB, during the load into the field in every run. During the menu visits the lows were 245-454 MiB: c2 245/306 MiB at visit 1, f2 287 MiB at visit 2, c1 351 MiB at the visit-3 close, f1 454 MiB at visit 1. The owner's failure had 1 MiB. The pool said 'short' 9-10 times per run. The reserve sat at 64 MiB from the load to the end in all four runs. The source counts the floor in 2 MiB blocks (EDEN_GUEST_RESERVE_FLOOR=32 means 32 blocks = 64 MiB; guest_memory_service.inc:160). So the reserve was exactly at its floor with nothing lendable. The exit-walk note's line that '64 is not its floor' is wrong; there too the reserve sat at the floor. The owner's crash had 28 blocks (56 MiB), under the floor. There were no lending lines. JIT memory grew in 26-27 steps of 16 MiB to 416-432 MiB (owner 512). The code piece that failed in the owner's crash reached 64 MiB here, the size it had at the crash, but every step was served because the pool still had pieces of 2 MiB and more. There was no 'JIT code cache' line. Flexible free was 91 MiB in all runs. In short, the reserve was at the same edge as at the crash, but the pool had 230+ MiB in one piece, so nothing had to wait or borrow.
