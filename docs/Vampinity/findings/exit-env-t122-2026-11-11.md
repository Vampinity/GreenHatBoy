Words, one line each:
- **pool**: the console's 12,352 MiB that the game's memory, our heap and the GPU driver all take from.
- **largest free block**: the biggest single free piece of the pool; a request bigger than it fails (crashes came at 0-2 MiB).
- **`low=`**: the census's lowest largest free block since its previous line (lines every ~2 s), so it catches dips between samples.
- **VMA**: the allocator that puts our GPU images and buffers into big blocks of driver memory.
- **slack**: block memory VMA holds that no image or buffer uses (holes left by deleted images).
- **heap**: the app's own memory for small requests; **arena**: one thread's own 2 MiB corner of it.
- **over-budget image**: an image VMA made although its budget figure was used up (a warning, not a refusal).
- **refusal**: an image or buffer the driver would not make for lack of memory.

Clocks: "s" is the census clock (`EDEN_MEM_CENSUS ... t_s=`). The script marks (`EDEN_TEST ... at=`) run 16.3-17.7 s
ahead of it in these runs (worked out per run from where the marks fall between census lines, to 0.1 s). The black
screen is the script's `GONE` wait (line 156, met when the hearts vanish) and matches the video's 1.1-1.2 s black.

## 4. Verdicts (one run per arm: none of this is a verdict on its own)

- **v1, VMA blocks of 8 MiB: worth a repeat.** It cuts driver slack by 120-250 MiB with no fps cost, the only
  memory effect in the batch beyond the control spread; whether that ever lifts the low is not seen yet.
  Repeat 3 runs v1 against 3 controls, alternating (c, v, c, v, c, v).
- **t1, VMA trim: neutral, parked behind v1.** Same slack cut as v1, plus churn and the batch's only cluster of
  over-budget images; retest only if v1's repeat shows the slack cut matters, then as v1 + t1 against v1.
- **k1, no kept arenas: retire as a memory lever.** It changed nothing in the heap (1 arena given back, idle
  arenas 2 MiB as in the controls), as the heap map predicts; its 12 MiB low came in the village, not from the
  switch. Removing KEEP_ARENAS stays a clean-up choice for the ledger, not a fix.

**The one number to track "how close to out of memory":** the census `low=` minimum over the whole play (hearts
to end), with its second, read as "N of M runs under 64 MiB" rather than as an average. Reasons: it is the only
figure that sees the 2 s dips (driver "available" and `largest=` miss them), it measures the thing that fails
(one free piece big enough), and the lows here came in the village as often as at the area change (k1 96 s,
c2 95.5 s), so a window around the black alone misses half of them. Its run-to-run spread is wide (57-96 at the
change between two identical controls), so any candidate needs 3+ runs per arm before it can be called.

## What this test could and could not see

- Could: all five reached the next area and stood 35+ s in it with its scene playing; env as planned; each switch
  read and acting; no refusals, no driver out-of-memory calls, no crash.
- Could not: the field (the route stops before it, where the one T121 crash was); any effect smaller than the
  control spread (40 MiB at the change, 350 MiB in the after-median); whether the fps and hitches differences
  of a few ms are more than chance; what t1's `trim=` and `fallback` fields mean exactly (read from the line
  only, not from source).
- c1 was the first run of the batch (save restored first); its flat 252 MiB cap after the change may be that,
  or chance. A batch that starts and ends with a control, as this one did, is the right shape; keep it.

## Check (second time)

Re-derived from the raw logs with my own scripts (scratch: `check2/p.py`, frames `check2/all.jpg`).
The verdicts hold. Five points to correct or add:

- **Confirmed.** All five runs ended `script_end`, and crash-index still ends at 060219. Each env.txt matches
  plan.json line for line, in order, plus TEST_BOOT and TEST_SCRIPT. Frames at -8, -1, +0.5, +3, +15 and +45 s
  around the black show the village, the passage, black, the next area and its scene in all five. Blackdetect: c1
  1.15 s, v1 **1.05**, t1 1.10, k1 1.18, c2 1.15. Each video has one ~0.6 s black at the end, not two. Area-change
  `low=` 57/34/32/38/96 and whole-play 57/34/32/12/68 match. So do the counts under 128/64, the heap POOL lines at
  the lows, slack medians (within 6 MiB), driver-available lows and the fps windows aligned on the black (eden_log
  time = `EDEN_GUEST_START` + `at=`).
- **Wrong v1 evidence.** "VMA heap 0 'blocks 8 MiB' where all others say 24" misreads the line.
  `VMA blocks N MiB` is heap 0's total block memory (`statistics.blockBytes`, vulkan_memory_allocator.cpp:1003),
  not the block size. All five runs print "VMA blocks 8 MiB" (c1 6x, t1 6x, k1 5x, c2 6x, v1 9x), and v1 also
  prints 16 and 32. The default block size is 16 MiB (vulkan_device.cpp:813), not 24. The real proof that the
  build reads it: vulkan_device.cpp:814-817 reads `EDEN_VMA_BLOCK_MIB` into `preferredLargeHeapBlockSize` (:857).
  The effect shows as driver app objects of up to 406 against 183-305 in the other runs, and as the slack cut.
- **The `EDEN_ENV` lines in heap.log prove nothing.** heap.log echoes every env line (85-87 per run), whether or
  not the build reads it, so "KEEP_ARENAS echoed" is not a read. `EDEN_HEAP_KEEP_ARENAS` is not in the T122 core
  source. The front's heap_arenas.inc:301-303 reads it (heap-pages-back/MAP.md). k1's proof of a read is its
  effect, `reused=1 given_back=1` against 0/0 in the four runs that keep arenas. t1's read is confirmed in source
  (vma_trim.h:28) and in the log (`EDEN_VMA_TRIM ready`, 69 lines).
- **Missed warning.** "No out-of-memory lines" is true only for the driver's `EDEN_RADV_OUT_OF_MEMORY`. The
  texture cache (the store of game images) logs `Out of device memory: keeping N MiB more free`. t1 has it 5 times,
  rising 512 -> 2560 MiB at eden_log 95.9-98.1 s (census ~61-63 s, the village load, together with its 15
  over-budget images). c1, v1 and k1 have it once each (512), c2 never. This adds to t1's cost signal.
- **Overstated, small.** "k1's 12 MiB low came in the village, not from the switch": one run cannot say that.
  It is the lowest low of the batch and below the earlier exit spread (16). It falls at the same seconds as the
  village dips in the other runs (95.5-99.1 s), so the route is the likely cause, but harm is not ruled out.
  Retiring k1 on the grounds that the heap did not change still stands. c1's village row also misses its
  `low=68` at 120.1 s (-18 s), between the two windows. In c2 the 96 MiB low prints just before the black mark
  (L1449 against L1452), while in the other runs it prints just after. Pairing lows across runs by the black is
  only exact to about 2 s.
